Clover Lawn for Dogs: What the Research Says

By Jon P. — founder of LawnSeedPicker and home-yard DIY enthusiast · Published November 22, 2026

No study shows that clover resists dog urine. Asked directly, University of Maine Extension said that despite plenty of claims online, it was "unable to find any support from sources I consider strongly evidence-based" for clover holding up better. What the research does support is a different plan: tall fescue as the grass, a little clover in it if you want, and stopping the spots before they start.

Does clover resist dog urine?

Not as far as anyone has shown. The University of Maine's answer to a reader who asked exactly this: "While there is a great deal of anecdotal evidence making the claim online," it could not find evidence-based support for clover being more resistant to yellowing — "So… maybe."

If a search result told you otherwise, check what it is quoting. One Ask Extension answer that often comes up for this question describes a turf as "resistant to dog urine" — but that line is about Dog Tuff, a sterile bermudagrass hybrid (Cynodon 'PWIN04S'), not clover.

What actually kills the grass

Spotting and dead grass are often blamed on the concentrated nitrogen and excess salts in dog urine, and the nitrogen is what makes the dark-green ring. But NC State Extension reports that "lactic acid has recently been identified as the main culprit for turfgrass death." Damage is worse when the soil is dry, when the grass isn't actively growing, and at higher temperatures, and damage from female dogs and puppies tends to be more severe because the urine lands in one spot.

The grass that holds up best: tall fescue

Grasses do differ. Among cool-season lawn grasses, NC State lists "tall fescue being most tolerant, followed by creeping red fescue, then perennial ryegrass, and finally Kentucky bluegrass." Bermudagrass and zoysiagrass are more tolerant than the cool-season grasses. One catch: tall fescue grows in clumps, so it "will not naturally fill in dead spots, and must be spot-seeded, plugged, or sodded."

So for a cool-season dog yard, the evidence points to turf-type tall fescue, with clover as an extra rather than the main plant. Maine Extension notes that clover is "very often mixed with some grass seed (commonly fescue)" rather than grown alone. For how much clover to blend in, see the clover to grass seed ratio. For seed, Envy turf-type tall fescue seed is one tall fescue option, and Outsidepride Miniclover seed is one clover option.

Clover under dog traffic

Dogs bring wear as well as urine. Maine Extension says: "Clover will tolerate some foot traffic, but I am hesitant to recommend it for heavy traffic areas." It also notes clover "tends to be quite slippery when wet and will crush more easily than turfgrass." In winter, the University of Maryland notes that "the topgrowth of microclover dies back in early winter and starts to regrow in early spring," and that seeding it too heavily can leave bare ground during that period. Clover flowers also draw bees, which Maine Extension flags as a hazard for bare feet.

What reduces spots, whatever you plant

NC State calls training your dog to use a mulched area, or the edge of the lawn, "the best way to stop urine damage." The other option is to water the spot within a few hours of the dog going; that limits the dead patch, though not the dark-green ring. For repairing spots that are already dead, see dog urine spots on the lawn. For other ground covers and dog runs, see the best ground cover for dogs.

Remedies that don't work

NC State does not recommend trying to neutralize urine with baking soda or gypsum, and says dietary supplements marketed to reduce urine damage "do not have any scientific merit and may negatively affect your dog's health." Ask your veterinarian before changing your dog's diet.

Frequently asked questions

Is clover resistant to dog urine?

No study shows it. University of Maine Extension found no evidence-based support for clover resisting yellowing from dog urine, despite the many claims online.

What is the best lawn for dogs?

Among cool-season grasses, NC State ranks tall fescue as the most tolerant of the lactic acid in dog urine, followed by creeping red fescue, perennial ryegrass and Kentucky bluegrass.

Will clover hold up to dogs running on it?

Some. Maine Extension says clover tolerates some foot traffic but is hesitant to recommend it for heavy traffic, and notes it is slippery when wet and crushes more easily than turfgrass.

Is clover safe for dogs?

None of the extension sources cited on this page addresses it. Ask your veterinarian.
